# Accounting model and conversion rules (schema 3)

This document defines the conversion before the implementation is run. Schema 1/2 accounts, transactions, categories, budgets and allocations remain in place. Existing IDs and foreign keys remain intact. New account roles distinguish assets from liabilities without rebuilding the existing accounts table or changing its legacy type constraint.

## Conversion

1. Map every existing account to a debit-normal asset ledger account. Map each category to its corresponding expense (debit-normal) or income (credit-normal) ledger account. Create a system opening-equity account.
2. Post each nonzero opening balance once against opening equity. Negative asset openings reverse the direction. A conversion key prevents duplicate posting on retry/restart.
3. For each historical expense, debit its category and credit its account. For income, debit the account and credit the category. For transfers, debit destination and credit source. Preserve transaction ID, date, description, notes and original payload.
4. Reconcile every existing account's derived Phase 1 balance against the ledger before marking conversion complete. Roll back conversion and migration together if reconciliation fails.
5. Historical opening dates were never recorded. The conversion assigns the earlier of account creation date and earliest associated transaction date; invalid legacy timestamps fall back to earliest transaction or conversion date. This is a documented reconstruction assumption. Net worth before an account's history boundary is explicitly incomplete; no older balance is invented.

Representative conversion expectations: opening ₹1,000 plus income ₹500 minus expense ₹100 and outgoing transfer ₹200 gives ₹1,200. Destination opening ₹50 plus transfer ₹200 gives ₹250. Income is ₹500 and expense ₹100, with no transfer income/expense. Conversion must be repeatable without additional journals.

## Posting and corrections

Each journal starts as draft, accepts integer INR debit/credit lines, and becomes posted only when at least two lines sum to equal positive debits and credits. SQLite triggers reject unbalanced posting, edits/deletion of posted lines, and edits/deletion of posted journal metadata. Every journal has a unique posting key. Currency must match its ledger accounts; currency conversion is not provided.

The transaction table remains an operational projection. Editing a transaction posts an equal/opposite reversal of its current journal at the original effective date, then posts the replacement at its new transaction date. Deletion voids the projection and posts a reversal; neither journal nor original transaction ID is deleted. Journal payloads preserve the previous values. Reports query the active transaction projection, which is rebuilt atomically with each balanced posting. Account balances and net worth are derived only from posted ledger lines. Reversals use original effective dates, so past statements/reports are restated; creation timestamps retain when the correction was made. This is not a locked-period accounting system.

Asset transfers, card payments, loan disbursements and loan principal repayments are balance-sheet movements. Card purchases, borrowed-loan interest and applicable expense fees affect expense categories once. Lent-loan interest/received fees are income. Loan payment principal, interest and fees are separate nonoverlapping transaction components, linked to a single loan-payment event and committed atomically. They do not duplicate the same amount.

Credit cards use credit-normal liabilities. Purchases credit the liability; payments debit it and credit the paying asset. Card opening amounts represent money owed (negative values represent a card credit). Available credit is limit minus outstanding; it is not clamped, so card credits or over-limit use remain visible. Interest/fees are entered explicitly. Statements are persisted period definitions with derived totals; minimum payments are optional supplied amounts, never guessed. Due day is the first occurrence of the configured day strictly after closing. Days beyond month length clamp to month end.

Recurring rules create scheduled occurrence records, not money movements. Daily/weekly dates use local calendar arithmetic. Monthly, quarterly and yearly dates retain the original start-day anchor and clamp missing days, so January 31 → February 28/29 → March 31. Confirming an occurrence posts once atomically; unique rule/date references prevent duplicates. Paused rules retain pending history. Cancellation stops future generation. Amount/category/account edits affect only unconfirmed occurrences; frequency/anchor changes after occurrence generation require cancelling and creating a new rule. Overdue occurrences remain available for explicit confirmation or skipping; generation is bounded in batches of 120. Background execution is not required or assumed.

Loans support borrowed/payable and lent/receivable roles, with personal/other labels. Interest is explicitly none, flat simple interest on original principal, or reducing simple interest on period-opening outstanding principal. Schedules use equal-principal installments, not an assumed annuity or compounded APR. Supported periods are monthly/quarterly/yearly; terms must contain whole periods. Rates are nominal annual percentages supplied by the user. Minor-unit interest rounds half up using integer arithmetic. Actual principal, interest and fees are entered explicitly. Extra/early principal changes outstanding immediately; recalculating the remaining schedule is a deliberate action and replaces only unpaid forecasts. Missed forecasts remain overdue; they do not auto-post or accrue penalties/interest. Repayments cannot exceed outstanding principal. Reversing a payment preserves its audit trail and makes schedule recalculation necessary.

Net worth is debit-normal asset balances minus credit-normal liability balances as of a local calendar date, including archived accounts and loan receivables/payables. Income/expense accounts are excluded. Currency groups are never combined. Only INR posting is supported currently. Percentage change is shown only when previous net worth is positive; otherwise it is undefined. Historical balances are reconstructed from posted lines, not editable snapshots.

## MVP v4 splits and refunds

Schema 5 adds `expense_splits` and transaction `refund_of_id`. Standard expense splits post one category debit per allocation and one account credit for their total. A linked refund posts category credits and an account debit, reducing asset outflow or card debt. Reports use negative expense amounts on the refund date, without treating returns as income. Refund capacity is enforced per original category across posted refunds; original purchases cannot be edited/voided until their active refunds are reversed. Corrections retain immutable journal history. `category_activity` centralizes allocated category amounts and `expected_transaction_lines` supports diagnostics. Savings goals/contributions and bill reminders are planning records and never post to the ledger.
